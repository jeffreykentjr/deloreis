# De Lore Is: material for the site

De Lore Is is a local continuity checker for fictional worlds. It records
structured claims and the decisions behind them, checks the constraints a
writer chooses, and can explain why a claim holds. Writers can work in `.dli`
files; assistants can use the MCP authoring tools. The checks are deterministic
and call no model.

A possible short description: **Continuity checking for fictional worlds.**

The audience is writers and worldbuilders with a growing series bible, teams
working in a shared setting, and people building tools that work with fiction.
DLI does not read a manuscript and find plot holes on its own. It checks the
facts and rules someone records; a writer decides what to accept.

The Holmes casebook gives the site a story to show. Watson's name demonstrates
a check against a single-value rule. The wound accounts remain unresolved: the
stories do not establish that the two references describe the same injury. The
Reichenbach example shows an earlier account, a later ruling, and the support
that changed. A branch keeps the earlier possibility. Each interpretation is
identified as a modelling choice and linked to the original stories.

Suggested overview: open with a concrete problem from a long-running world;
show the Watson name check and the writer's choice; explain sourced claims,
revision, and the local command-line and MCP interfaces; then link to the
quickstart, casebook, and wiki. Avoid presenting an interpretation of Holmes
as a definitive textual contradiction.

Possible answer pages:

- How can I keep continuity straight in a long series?
- How can I record competing accounts without choosing canon yet?
- How do I revise a story fact without losing its history?
- Can an assistant turn my series-bible notes into a checked world?
- Why do the Holmes stories describe Watson's wound differently?
