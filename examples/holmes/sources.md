# Sources for the Holmes model

The examples cite Conan Doyle's stories directly. Each decision ID below names
a source passage or an editorial ruling; citing a passage records what the
narrator says, which may be wrong. The casebook marks the model's own
interpretations as it makes them.

| Decision | Text | What the passage says |
|---|---|---|
| `d-0001` | [*A Study in Scarlet*, part I, chapter 1](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/244/244-h/244-h.htm#link2HCH0001) | The title names John H. Watson. Watson says he was “struck on the shoulder by a Jezail bullet” at Maiwand. |
| `d-0002` | [*The Sign of the Four*](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/2097/2097-h/2097-h.htm) | Watson is “nursing my wounded leg” and says a Jezail bullet went through it. The text does not establish that this is the same wound as the one at Maiwand. |
| `d-0003` | [*The Man with the Twisted Lip*](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/1661/1661-h/1661-h.htm#chap06) | Watson's wife offers to send “James” to bed in a conversation in which Watson is present. The text does not explicitly say James is his legal first name. |
| `d-0004` | [*The Final Problem*](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/834/834-h/834-h.htm#chap12) | Watson concludes that Holmes and Moriarty died at Reichenbach. |
| `d-0005` | [*The Empty House*](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/108/108-h/108-h.htm#chap01) | Holmes returns and says of the chasm, “I never was in it.” |
| `d-0006` | Editorial modelling choice | Records the era ordering, the single-first-name constraint, and the example rule relating a name used by a spouse to a first name. |
| `d-0007` | [*A Scandal in Bohemia*](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/1661/1661-h/1661-h.htm#chap01) | Dates Watson’s married visit to Holmes to 20 March 1888. |
| `d-0008` | Editorial ruling based on *The Final Problem* | Accept Watson’s conclusion that Holmes died, as an initial ruling. |
| `d-0009` | Editorial ruling based on *The Empty House* | Supersede that ruling after Holmes returns. |

The unresolved wound claims use the proposal support labels
`h.study-in-scarlet` and `h.sign-of-four` for `d-0001` and `d-0002`. They are
labels for this example's accounts, not decision citations.

The single-wound and single-first-name rules are exercises: the model asks what
follows *if* you adopt them. Watson's conclusion about Reichenbach is modelled
as an account that a later story revises.

For chronology, [*A Scandal in Bohemia*](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/1661/1661-h/1661-h.htm#chap01) places Watson married by 20 March 1888. [*The Sign of the Four*](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/2097/2097-h/2097-h.htm#chap03) describes a September evening when Watson is with Mary Morstan. She dates an advertisement to 4 May 1882, about six years before her visit; her father disappeared in December 1878, nearly ten years before the visit; and she has received six yearly pearls. Together these point toward September 1888. The year and the identity of Watson's wife are our readings.

In *The Sign of the Four*, the wound and Holmes's monographs appear in
[chapter 1](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/2097/2097-h/2097-h.htm#chap01);
Mary's account is in [chapter 2](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/2097/2097-h/2097-h.htm#chap02);
and the Sholto family account is in [chapter 4](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/2097/2097-h/2097-h.htm#chap04).
Jonathan Small and Tonga appear later in the same story.
